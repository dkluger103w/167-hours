Capture, not coaching

Most tools ask you to talk their way. We start with how you already talk.

When people reach for AI in a hard moment, they don't open a worksheet. They type or say whatever is on their mind, in their own words, often in pieces across a whole week. A two-line check-in on Monday. A long vent on Thursday night. One sentence in the car.

Most mental health apps are built around a different idea: that you'll come to them, follow their structure, and fill in their format.

Guided journals

You answer daily prompts, fill in templates, and rate your mood. Some produce a report you can share.

What's missingIt feels like homework. Most people don't journal on a schedule, and the moments that matter rarely arrive on time.

Exercise programs

A chatbot walks you through structured exercises, like reframing a thought or a breathing routine, often assigned as modules.

What's missingUseful practice, but it steers the conversation. It answers the question it was built for, not the one you came with.

AI "therapists"

An AI positioned as the therapy itself, always available. Some remember you between chats, but the conversations often feel scripted, guiding you through the app's own session format.

What's missingNothing reaches a real clinician. When things get serious, there's no one in the loop.

Closed networks

Online therapy services, health plans, and large provider groups are adding AI between visits, some with clinicians supervising. The closest to what we believe in.

What's missingIt's a closed system. It only works if you're their client, seeing their therapist, under their plan. If your therapist is outside it, you're out of luck.

167 Hours

You're not the only one

If you've ever poured your heart out to a chatbot at 2am, you're in good company.

Using AI between sessions doesn't mean you're doing therapy wrong.

Millions of people already talk to AI about what they're going through. It isn't strange, and it's nothing to hide. When things get hard, people reach for whatever is there, and late at night that's usually the phone in their hand.

Here's the honest reality: this technology is here, and it isn't going anywhere. People are going to use it whether anyone approves or not. We think that's okay. Talking things through, even with an AI, can genuinely help.

If your therapist were available around the clock, you'd just talk to them. But no therapist can be there 24 hours a day. So the conversations happen anyway, and then they disappear. The thing you finally put into words on Thursday night. The urge you got through on Saturday. The pattern you started to notice. Your therapist never hears any of it.

We're not here to police what you say or steer the conversation. Talk about whatever you need to, the way you already do. 167 Hours pulls out what matters and gets it to the person who can actually help you work through it.

The AI is there at 2am. Your therapist is who helps you make sense of it.

Where we're headed

A hybrid model, built with clinicians.

These are the concepts we're building toward.

Concept

The loop

Other tools give therapists a window into the AI. We're building toward a loop. The AI is there in the 167 hours your therapist can't be, and clinicians help shape and improve it over time.

Concept

Two human layers

Your therapist knows you and receives your summaries. Behind the AI, a team of clinical specialists reviews how it's working and coaches it. They're also the people who step in when you raise your hand. They stay anonymous and never know who you are.

Concept

Raise your hand

A real person when you need one, without waiting for your next session.

Once a week doesn't always feel like enough. But a therapist on call for every person, 24 hours a day, isn't possible. If 10,000 people each needed one-to-one coverage around the clock, the cost would put it out of reach for almost everyone.

So we're building something different: a shared team of clinical specialists, on shift around the clock. Most of the time, nobody needs them. When someone does, one of them can step in.

  • You raise your hand. Something feels too big for a machine and you want a real person's take. A specialist reviews what you shared and joins the conversation. Maybe not in a minute, but you won't be waiting days for your next appointment.
  • The system raises it for you. If a conversation shows signs of real concern, it's flagged to the team, and a specialist can step in.
  • They don't know who you are. Specialists see the moment, not your name. They're anonymous to you, and you're anonymous to them.
  • Your therapist stays your therapist. Online therapy services match you with one therapist and sell you their time. This doesn't replace your therapist or sell you more sessions. It adds a layer of human backup between visits, shared across everyone, which is what keeps it affordable.

Raise your hand is not an emergency service. If you're in crisis, call or text 988, or call 911.

For therapists

Start the session where it matters.

Your clients are already talking to AI between sessions. Most of it never reaches you.

167 Hours gives you a short, structured summary before each session: what happened and when, what came up most, any urges and how they went, and what your client wants to talk about. It's not a clinical assessment and never makes a diagnosis. All clinical judgment stays with you.

Your privacy

Your conversations belong to you.

  • Stored securely, tied to your account. What you share lives in a protected database that belongs to your account, not in a chat log you'll never see again.
  • You decide what your therapist sees. Say "keep this out of my notes," and it stays out. It's saved for you and never shared.
  • The same goes for every clinician. Nothing reaches your therapist, or anyone else, without your OK.
  • Anonymous by design. In our future model, clinical specialists who help improve the AI would never see who you are.
  • Built toward end-to-end encryption and full health-privacy standards, so your words stay readable by you and the people you choose. We'll have these in place before any clinician works inside the platform.
Why we built this

Therapy works. It just happens one hour a week.

We saw a gap that kept showing up from both sides of the room. Clients are already turning to AI between sessions, often in their hardest moments, and none of it reaches the person trained to help. Therapists start each session catching up instead of moving forward.

167 Hours was designed to close that gap. It was shaped by years of experience on the client side of therapy, input from clinicians, including a therapist who ran his own practice, and a background in building technology people rely on every day. The goal from the start was to give the therapist the full picture, never to replace them.

Help first. We're not building this to profit from people who are struggling. Our goal is to keep it affordable and accessible to anyone in therapy.

Questions

Good to know.

Is this therapy?

No. It supports the therapy you already have. You need a licensed therapist to use it.

What if I'm in crisis?

167 Hours is not a crisis service. If you're in danger or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, US) or call 911.

How do I actually use it?

You talk to it the same way you'd talk to ChatGPT or Claude: type or speak, whenever you want, about whatever is on your mind. It runs on the same leading AI models millions of people already use, so it will feel familiar. The difference is what happens after: what matters is organized and kept safe for your therapist. We're in early testing now, and our early group gets access first.

Does the AI give advice, or just listen?

It talks with you naturally, the way a good listener would. It won't run you through a program, assign exercises, or tell you what to do with your life. That's your therapist's role.

Who can see what I write?

You, and the therapist you choose to share with. You decide what goes into their summary. Our anonymous clinical specialists only get involved when you raise your hand or something is flagged as concerning, and they never see your name.

Is my data used to train AI?

We don't use your conversations to train AI models. We build on the business versions of leading AI services, which don't train on customer conversations by default.

Will my therapist actually read it?

It's designed to be read in a few minutes before a session: a short, structured summary, not a transcript. We're working with therapists now to make sure it shows what they actually want to see.

Does my therapist have to sign up?

Not to start. You can share your summary with any therapist you already see.

What if I stop using it?

Your story is yours. You'll be able to download everything you've shared or delete it completely.

What does it cost?

We're keeping it as affordable as possible. Pricing will be shared with our early group first.

Who's behind this?

A small team building this mission first: people who know what it's like to be the client, working alongside clinicians. Our goal is to help as many people as possible, not to maximize profit.
